A B S T R A C T
Proton exchange membrane fuel cell (PEMFC) is regarded as one of the leading developmental directions of the 
green power source. Predicting the remaining useful life (RUL) of online PEMFCs is challenging, primarily due to 
factors such as complex nonlinear degradation patterns and unknown data. To enhance the performance of RUL 
prediction, we present a deep learning model-based temporal fusion transformer model to predict the future total 
voltage of a PEMFC stack. The performance of RUL predictions was evaluated using different starting RUL 
prediction points to compare them with the existing study. The experiments are conducted with different future 
prediction steps for future RUL prediction to determine the model’s prediction step limit. The proposed method 
achieved relative errors of 0.0148 %, 0.0060 %, 0.0662 %, and 0.0486 % for training lengths 300, 400, 500, and 
600, respectively, in the FC1 and 0.0326 % in the FC2 datasets compared to the existing study scenarios. The 
experimental analysis resulted in relative errors of 0.0738 % for the FC1 dataset, 1.0239 % and 1.6512 % for the 
two FC2 dataset scenarios.
1. Introduction
The proton exchange membrane fuel cell (PEMFC) is a multi- 
variable, nonlinear system including multi-physics, multi-scale, and 
multiphase, which is challenging to model dynamically. Because of its 
high density, eco-friendly, and lightweight, PEMFC has become a critical 
power source for many transportation applications [1]. Therefore, 
managing PEMFC is essential by accurately forecasting the remaining 
useful life (RUL) prediction and making relevant optimal operational 
decisions. Recently, the data-driven approach based on the deep 
learning (DL) algorithm has been widely used for solving nonlinear and 
multi-characteristic modeling problems. The most popular structure is 
the recurrent neuron network (RNN) for predicting the RUL of PEMFC 
because of recurrent weight updating in recurrent cells [2]. To the best 
of our knowledge, making an accurate RUL prediction of dynamic 
PEMFC operation is challenging because of its variety of degradation 
patterns. The RNN and echo state network (ESN) models for single input 
RUL prediction developed for PEMFC static operating conditions have 
yet to consider the voltage recovery effect caused by the stack test 
start-stop [3]. The degradation of stack voltage decreases monotonically 
in static operating conditions. Since the voltage is readily observable in 
the PEMFC, it serves as a health indicator [4]. The reactant voltage, 
current, reactant temperature, and pressure of PEMFC contain impor­
tant degradation information. Existing data-driven methods require 
large amounts of aging data to achieve accurate decay trend prediction 
for PEMFC. Using multivariate time series data, single-input prediction 
algorithms cannot accurately predict RUL under dynamic PEMFC 
operating conditions. Yue et al. [5] provided a good review on analyzing 
the prognostics scales, horizon, threshold, and methods such as 
data-driven, model-based, and hybrid methods for the RUL prediction of 
PEFMC. Zhang et al. [6] reviewed the new development of the state of 
health (SOH) prediction of PEMFC systems, including the diagnostic 
techniques, the health indicator (HI) approaches, and the innovative 
application of effectiveness of these techniques and approaches. They 
pointed out that these studies play a role in extending the service life of 
the PEMFC systems after completing the judgment of the SOH of the 
stack system.
Tao et al. [7] proposed a power consisting of a two-dimensional 
convolutional neural network (CNN2D), improved Manta-Ray foraging 
optimization (MRFO), and gate recurrent unit (GRU), the full hybrid DL 
model called CNN2D-MRFO-GRU. Using CNN2D to extract features from 
the nonlinear degradation characteristics and combine them with the 
optimal GRU model, which is optimized with an improved MRFO al­
gorithm. The hybrid model outperformed the others compared models

(BiLSTM) model with multiple variables has been used to predict the 
RUL of PEMFC, where Gaussian filtering and cubic interpolation are 
used to preprocess the data [8]. A hybrid method, including the residual 
block, the LSTM model, and the multi-head self-attention, is proposed to 
predict the total voltage degradation [9]. When fuel cells are under

hybrid method based on the convolutional neural network (CNN) and

model is used to smooth the data [13].
The existing data-driven methods cannot accurately predict the RUL 
of PEMFC under dynamic operating conditions and require substantial 
training data. In addition, most studies used all data to validate their 
approaches. The RUL prediction deals with future forecasts. Only a few 
existing researches discuss the step-ahead prediction for future RUL 
prediction. That is, the data after the RUL prediction point are unknown. 
Jin et al. [14] introduced a cycle reservoir with a jump with a 
multi-input and multi-outputs (MIMO-CRJ) framework to enhance 
training efficiency and simplify the ESN model. They introduced the 
concepts of single-step and multi-step prediction. Multi-step predictions 
are attained through an iterative application of single-step predictions. It 
should be noted that the ESN model is not a generative model trained 
through sequence-to-sequence learning; hence, multi-step prediction 
relies on using the previously predicted value for subsequent pre­
dictions. Benaggoune et al. [15] introduced a multi-step-ahead predic­
tion structure based on a dilated CNN with an attention mechanism for 
PEMFC. Their research compared the step-ahead predictions at steps 
five, ten, fifteen, and twenty-five with other RNN-based models. Only a 
few existing papers focus on predicting the future RUL for PEMFC. In 
addition, we should address the issue of future prediction steps. If the 
model can accurately predict more future steps, the operator or user can 
detect degradation for the next step earlier. This research aims to 
determine the prediction horizon limit for the deep learning model when 
predicting the RUL.
In recent years, generative artificial intelligence (AI) has gained 
popularity and is expected to be a trend in the next generation, exem­
plified by models like transformers and generative pre-trained trans­
formers (GPTs) [16]. The temporal fusion transformer (TFT) model has 
been successfully applied in time series analysis [17]. This model em­
ploys sequence-to-sequence learning, using past input features and 
known future features to predict the next step in the time series data. In 
this research, the hour index serves as the known feature, and multiple 
hour indices input to perform multi-step ahead predictions. As deep 
learning models become increasingly complex and accurate, researchers 
are working on enhancing the model’s interpretability to explain the 
contributions of each input feature, thus improving the performance of 
the deep learning model. The TFT model includes variable selection 
networks that calculate and understand feature contributions. The TFT 
model has the advantage of learning long-term dependencies using a 
self-attention mechanism; this means it can directly learn patterns 
during the training process, giving the model more advantage over many 
deep learning models in terms of robustness and stability.
This study modified the TFT model by integrating a BiLSTM encoder- 
decoder layer for future RUL prediction of PEMFCs. Compared to the 
original TFT model, the improved version demonstrates superior per­
formance and effectively addresses the challenges of future RUL pre­
diction at each prediction step in our experimental analysis. Our 
proposed approach incorporates data preprocessing methods, including 
the Bacon-Watts and double Bacon-Watts models [18], for defining the 
training set and feature selection using a random forest (RF) model. The 
knee-onset and knee-point identified by the Bacon-Watts and double 
Bacon-Watts models serve as the training and prediction starting points 
(Sp). To optimize the hyperparameters of the deep learning model, we 
employ an open-source AutoML toolkit alongside the improved TFT 
model in the proposed approach. During RUL prediction, our approach 
supports online prediction, enabling real-time forecasting when PEMFC 
indicators are unavailable at the time of prediction. To emphasize the 
issues addressed in this paper, our proposed approach for online RUL 
prediction differs from traditional SOH estimation, which assumes that 
input variables are fully known during prediction. In summary, the 
proposed approach motivated by some limitations of DL models for 
online RUL prediction concerning long-term and local dependency 
learning ability, future prediction steps, and the nonlinearity relation­
ship between the PEMFC HI and input features, a deep learning 
model-based improved TFT is developed with tree-structure Parzen 
estimator (TPE) involved in an open-source AutoML for hyperparameter 
tuning and quantile prediction interval for uncertainty estimation. The 
main contributions and novelties of this paper are: 
1. The original TFT model’s LSTM encoder component was replaced 
with a BiLSTM encoder to capture and extract both forward and 
backward sequence features.
2. The Bacon-Watts and double Bacon-Watts models define the training 
set’s significant battery degradation trend to improve the DL model’s 
performance on future RUL prediction problems.
3. A detailed discussion of feature selection using an RF model based on 
feature domain visualization helps the reader understand its 
importance.
4. This research employs an open-source AutoML toolkit to fine-tune 
the hyperparameters using the TPE algorithm and visualize the 
convergence of hyperparameter optimization for the improved TFT 
model.
5. Different prediction steps of the improved TFT model for future RUL 
prediction are demonstrated to determine its prediction limits and 
comprehensively analyze its predictive behavior in the experimental 
analysis.
The overall organization of the paper is as follows. Section 2 presents 
the dataset and RUL definition. Section 3 describes the proposed method 
and its implementation. Section 4 provides the experimental validation 
for the proposed improved TFT prediction method and further results 
analysis. The conclusion is presented in Section 5.
2. Dataset description
2.1. Row dataset introduction
To verify the performance of the proposed approach in PEMFC online 
RUL prediction, we selected one of the most widely used PEMFC 
degradation datasets from the IEEE PHM 2014 Data Challenge [19] for 
experimental analysis. FC1 and FC2, which consist of two long-term 
PEMFC stack experiments with 5-cell stacks and an active area of 100 
cm2, were chosen to validate the proposed approach. FC1 was tested 
under stationary conditions in a current of 70A, and each character­
ization was measured every week at times 0, 48,185, 348, 515, 658, 823, 
and 991 h. FC2 was operated under a dynamic ripple current of 70A with 
7A oscillations at a frequency of 5 kHz, and the characterization was 
estimated every week at 0, 35, 182, 343, 515, 666, 830, and 1016 h. 
Twenty-five characterizations for FC1 and FC2 datasets to monitor the 
battery’s status and prognostic during each measured time interval are 
shown in Table 1. Compared to other prognostic applications, publicly 
available datasets for PEMFC prognostics are limited. Therefore, we 
focus on FC1 and FC2 data from the IEEE PHM 2014 Data Challenge, the 
only publicly available open-source dataset. To enhance dataset di­
versity, we designed three different experimental scenarios, each

Understanding which characteristics best represent battery degra­
dation is crucial before conducting experimental analysis. Based on 
previous studies, several researchers have emphasized that the most 
essential and significant characteristic in the FC1 and FC2 datasets is the 
degradation of PEMFC total voltage [15]. The total voltage is the sum of 
the voltages of all individual cells connected in series within the cell 
pack. The feature of Vtotal represents the total voltage in both datasets, 
which performs the polarization of stacked cell degradation. Most 
studies analyze Vtotal as the response variable for PEMFC RUL prediction 
[16]. Thus, in this study, the characterization of Vtotal is considered the 
response variable, while the other features serve as independent vari­
ables in the proposed approach.
2.2. PEMFC end of life determination
Determining a suitable end of life (EOL) threshold is essential in the 
RUL prediction. In the real world, the EOL determination is based on the 
application. If the application requires the PEMFC charge-discharge low 
frequency, the EOL threshold would be defined as a high value to renew 
the PEMFC when its degradation to the threshold early. This paper in­
vestigates the related papers using the same dataset with us for the RUL 
prediction. The investigation results for EOL determination are shown in 
Table 2. Based on Table 2 EOL threshold reference, this study chooses 
the EOL threshold as 96 % of the initial Vtotal. However, the EOL may 
differ even if the same threshold is selected because of different data 
preprocessing or personal determination. In Fig. 1, two points are lower 
than the chosen threshold of the FC1 total voltage degradation curve. 
This paper assumes that the first point drop to 96 % is the EOL of FC. 
Finally, the EOL is determined as 815 h for the FC1 dataset, which is 
reached to other research and can compare the RUL prediction results.
This research uses the same EOL threshold as 96 % of the initial Vtotal 
to identify the EOL hours for FC2. However, determining the EOL hour 
can be challenging due to the variability of the Vtotal degradation curve, 
as demonstrated in Fig. 1. Table 3 shows two different EOL hour 
determination criteria based on references for the FC2 dataset. In the 
first scenario, some studies select the EOL hours to exceed 500, and the 
corresponding training length is more than 500. Conversely, in the 
second scenario, other studies set the EOL hour to be less than 500, with 
training durations less than 500 h.
Following their respective logic, this research establishes two sce­
narios: if the EOL hours index exceeds 500 h, the training duration 
should exceed 500 h; otherwise, it should be less than 500 h. In this 
study, experiments utilize the first and last data points, representing a 
96 % capacity drop, for EOL times of 393 and 878 h for FC2 under both 
scenarios. These EOL hours were selected because they are consistent 
with prior research, allowing for a comparative analysis of RUL pre­
diction performance.

3.1. Raw data analysis
After getting the EOL point by the EOL threshold, the Bacon-Watts 
model and the double Bacon-Watts model calculate the knee-point and 
knee-onset of the Vtotal curve to determine the training length of offline 
model training. The training set is the period between knee point and 
onset. The reason for choosing the Bacon-Watts model and the double 
Bacon-Watts model to define the training set for the battery online RUL 
prediction problem is that the knee-point identified by the Bacon-Watts 
model indicates when the degradation trend begins to decline sharply. In 
contrast, the knee-onset point detected by the double Bacon-Watts 
model provides an early warning before the degradation trend acceler­
ates. These two models help define the training set by removing 
redundant stable trends, reducing data interference, and enhancing 
model performance for online RUL prediction of PEMFC degradation 
aging. This period effectively represents the accelerated PEMFC degra­
dation process due to the mathematical definitions of the knee-point and 
knee-onset (see the following equations).
Equation (1) represents the Bacon-Watts model for knee-point esti­
mation, and Equation (2) is the double Bacon-Watts model, which is 
modified from the Bacon-Watts model for estimating the knee-onset.

5. Conclusions
Predicting the online RUL of the PEMFC pack is challenging, espe­
cially under dynamic operation conditions, but the proposed approach 
with improved TFT model can tackle it. This paper analyzes explicitly 
the PEMFC dataset and predicts the health indicator of Vtoal through 
three analysis parts: raw data analysis, modeling, and future RUL pre­
diction. In raw data analysis, the suitable interpolation approach is used 
to remove outliers and knee-onset and knee-point identification to 
determine the training set for the proposed model. The RF model selects 
essential features at the end of raw data analysis. This research uses 
PCA’s feature domain analysis to verify and visualize that our raw data 
analysis is reasonable and makes sense. For modeling, the determined 
training set is used to train the proposed improved TFT model, which 
changes the encoder module from LSTM to BiLSTM. During model 
training, this research combines with the NNI AutoML toolkit to opti­
mize the hyperparameters. The toolkit provides hyperparameter 
converge process visualization, which can easily understand the opti­
mization process. After the proposed model training, the train model 
with optimal hyperparameters is used for online RUL prediction.
This study builds one scenario for the FC1 dataset and two scenarios 
for the FC2 dataset by determining the different EOL by ourselves, four 
different training lengths for the FC1 dataset, and one for the FC2 
dataset to compare the model performance with other existing research. 
The training length determination of comparison follows the existing 
research. The online RUL results of the proposed improved TFT model 
demonstrate under static (FC1 dataset) conditions of the comparison 
with other research shows that the RE of the online RUL prediction 
performance of the improved TFT model for training data [0,300 h], 
[0,400 h], [0,500 h], and [0,600 h] are 0.0148 %, 0.0060 %, 0.0662 %, 
and 0.0486 %, respectively. Under dynamic operation (FC2 dataset) 
conditions of the comparison with other research, the RE of the online 
RUL prediction performance of the improved TFT model for training 
data [0,300 h] is 0.0326 %. The experimental results demonstrate that 
the RE is lower than 0.07 % at different training durations and has better 
prediction performance than ESN, LSTM, and BiLSTM. In our build 
experiment, the online RUL prediction RE values for the three experi­
ments are 0.0738 %, 1.0239 %, and 1.6512 %, respectively.
The most important finding in this study is that we tested five 
different multi-step-ahead parameters to determine the prediction 
capability limits of the proposed improved TFT model. Through these 
experiments, we found that the improved TFT model can provide reli­
able five-step-ahead predictions with minimal accuracy loss and low 
prediction uncertainty, as verified through testing across various pre­
diction steps. Additionally, this study employs PCA to visualize the 
domain differences between models with and without feature selection, 
highlighting the importance of feature selection in deep learning model 
training. Our proposed approach’s analysis procedure integrates multi­
ple methodological components, which collectively enhance the future 
prediction performance of the improved TFT model. The outcomes of 
each experiment highlight that the proposed approach, designed for 
robustness and high reliability in online RUL prediction, can effectively 
address real-world PEMFC future prediction challenges. In the future, 
we will explore the application of the improved TFT model in transfer 
learning for PEMFC lifetime prediction. Due to the scarcity of battery 
degradation data, transfer learning can adapt model weights from 
source battery data to target battery data, enabling reliable predictions 
with limited target data. We will focus on domain adaptation, a widely 
used transfer learning technique that mitigates domain shifts between 
source and target datasets. Specifically, we aim to address temporal 
feature disparities and optimize model weights to align with the target 
domain. This approach will enhance future RUL predictions while 
reducing the data collection effort for researchers.
