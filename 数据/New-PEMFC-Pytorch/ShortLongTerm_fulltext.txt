A short- and long-term prognostic associating with remaining useful life 
estimation for proton exchange membrane fuel cell

H I G H L I G H T S 
• Five multi-step strategies are compared to implement short-term voltage prognostic. 
• Variable-step procedures are proposed to forecast the long-term degradation trend. 
• Based on the evolutionary algorithm, the hyper-parameters adjustment is proposed. 
• Remaining useful life is evaluated under the data-driven prognostics framework.

A B S T R A C T  
Proton exchange membrane fuel cell (PEMFC), as a promising power source, provides a feasible solution for clean 
and low-carbon energy systems. The durability problem restricts PEMFC application in some scenarios, which 
can be improved by the prognostic technology indirectly. This paper aims to develop a data-based method to 
implement the short-term and long-term prognostic simultaneously, and the developed long-term prognostic can 
be performed without future operation information. First, the short-term prognostics of five multi-step ahead 
forecasting strategies are proposed and compared based on a long short-term memory (LSTM) network. Results 
show that the multi-step input and multi-step output (MIMO) with LSTM strategy has a better performance in the 
short-term prognostics under the test conditions of the stationary and dynamic current. Then, the hyper- 
parameters of the prediction model are determined by an evolutionary algorithm. Furthermore, in the long- 
term prognostics regime, the variable-step long-term method is proposed and rectified by the short-term prog­
nostics. Finally, the developed remaining useful life (RUL) prediction is compared with a model-based extended 
Kalman filter. The average root mean square error results for the short-term prognostics of two conditions are 
0.00532 and 0.00538, respectively. The RUL estimations of two PEMFCs named FC1 and FC2 are given with 95% 
and 90% confidence intervals, respectively. Consequently, the proposed method can achieve acceptable accu­
racies in the short-term prognostic, the long-term prognostic, and the RUL prediction.   
1. Introduction 
As an environmentally friendly power system with high efficiency, 
the proton exchange membrane fuel cell (PEMFC) has been treated as 
one of the most promising power sources that is gradually applied in the 
automobile, stationary product, and portable power areas [1]. However, 
the material degradation in each component of the PEMFC may result in 
the shortage of its life span, which constrains the deployment and 
commercialization. Prognostic is the core technique in the prognostics 
and health management (PHM) process, which helps to monitor the 
state of health and estimate the remaining useful life (RUL) of PEMFC 
[2]. Consequently, even if material degradation is inevitable, an accu­
rate prognostic can provide a foundation of decision-making and control 
to decelerate the aging process. From different perspectives, prognostics 
can be divided into short- and long-term prognostic. The short one can 
catch the local variation with high accuracy, while the long-term 
prognostic can capture the aging trend. Here, the PEMFC output 
voltage as a readily available parameter is chosen to evaluate the degree 
of aging. And the prognostics are conducted without prior knowledge of 
the future information. Generally, the prognostics of PEMFCs can be 
distinguished into three approaches: data-based methods, model-based

1.1. Literature review 
Machine learning technique, as one of the data-based methods, is 
introduced as the black-box model to describe the aging process of the 
PEMFC. An estimator of solid oxide fuel cells based on a neural network 
has been developed to predict voltage variation due to the degradation 
[4]. In multi-scale prediction, Silva et al. [5] proposed a methodology 
based on adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference systems to achieve short, me­
dium, or long-term predictions. However, this method represents a 
relatively high error in short-term prediction. Echo state networks (ESN) 
[6,7] were introduced into the prognostics of the PEMFC together with a 
filtering method and multi-input parameters, respectively. The prog­
nostics method based on the relevance vector machine (RVM) was 
proposed in [8] to improve the accuracy in short-term forecasting, 
which has been validated in the single-step-ahead and multi-step-ahead 
prediction. Furthermore, a modified RVM was then proposed to meet the 
condition of relatively small training data sets [9]. Besides, pattern 
recognition [10] has exhibited the effectiveness of the PEMFC prog­
nostic. Zhou et al. [11] proposed a hierarchical degradation prognostic 
approach to guarantee the robustness and prediction accuracy. Liu et al. 
[12] proposed a short-term prognostics method based on the group 
method of data handling and the wavelet analysis to achieve high ac­
curacy in different load conditions. An adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference 
system with a fuzzy c-means strategy was applied for the same purpose 
in [13], which was training with the raw data. Besides, this approach 
combing with the automatic parameter adjustment has been verified on 
an experimental platform. An ensemble ESN was implemented to 
enhance the prognostic adaptability [14]. Based on the virtual steady- 
state stack voltage health indicator, an acceptable prognostic could be 
achieved in the long-term prediction horizon. Chen et al. [15] have 
proposed a novel degradation prognosis based on the wavelet neural 
network and Cuckoo search algorithm. This method considering the 
main operating conditions could be applied to the health state prog­
nostic of PEMFC under different conditions. Xie et al [16] developed a 
hybrid neural network combining the deep belief network and extreme 
learning machine with the actual data validation. A digital twin tech­
nique has been extended to the energy domain to establish an ensemble 
RUL prediction system [17]. To improve the data processing ability, the 
attention mechanism helps to extract the useful feature [18] and a self- 
optimization regression method is introduced to prognose the degra­
dation trend [19]. To enhance the reliability, He et al. [20] proposed a 
long-term prediction method based on a least absolute shrinkage and 
selection operator-echo state network together with a model selection 
rule. And the final prediction was defined as an average value of the 
prediction results. 
The model-based approach is always used to describe the long-term 
aging process of PEMFC combining empirical and physical models. 
Jouin et al. [21] have proposed three empirical models, including linear, 
logarithmic, and exponential models to approximate the aging process. 
And the prognostic was conducted under a particle filtering framework 
to estimate the RUL of the PEMFC. Furthermore, the degradation model 
has been derived from the polarization curve by introducing two in­
dicators of the state of health [22]. The extended Kalman filter (EKF) has 
helped to forecast the future performance and RUL of the fuel cell. More 
health indicators have been proposed. Zhou et al. [23] presented a 
multi-physical aging model, considering ohmic losses, reaction activity 
losses, and mass transfer losses. A prognostics framework composed of a 
parameter extraction process and the particle filtering method was 
proposed which realized the RUL prediction at a constant operating 
condition [24]. Zhu et al. [25] applied the Gaussian process state space 
to infer the internal time-varying parameters of the dynamic model. In 
addition, an observer-based prognostic algorithm based on the EKF was 
applied to estimate the actual state of health under variable loads and 
verify the reliability by quantifying the uncertainty [26]. From the other 
perspective, Jha et al. [27] developed a special model of PEMFC by using 
Bond Graph theory, which presented a simplified graphical represen­
tation of the complex physics of PEMFC and efficient decomposition 
based on the functionality. The prognostics problem can be formulated 
as the joint state-parameter estimation problem in the particle filter 
framework where the estimation is obtained in the probabilistic domain 
as well. However, the stack voltage is only related to degradation 
symptoms and is significantly affected by operating conditions. To 
improve the prognostics accuracy, the approach coupling of two nu­
merical models concerning the fuel cell operation and surface degra­
dation has been investigated in [28]. Chen et al. [29] selected the stack 
voltage and power to construct a fusion health indicator. Then, a 2nd 
order Gaussian degradation model was built to complete the RUL 
prognostics based on an unscented particle filter. Two different physics- 
based models were used for the objective of combining the stack voltage 
and the stack state of health as a fusion health indicator, and an

The hybrid approaches are the integration of the previous two

obtained by the data-based method. Moreover, a brand-new perspective 
of the hybrid degradation model was raised in [36]. The voltage aging 
waveform of PEMFC was decomposed into multiple sub-waveforms by 
the wavelet analysis. Then, each sub-waveform could be characterized 
by the extreme learning machine. The moving window technique was 
used to adjust the fusion weight factors of model-based and data-based 
approaches adaptively. This method could capture the long-term 
degradation trend, and the local voltage variation characteristics as 
well [37]. Xie et al. [38] proposed a fusion prognostic approach based on 
particle filter and LSTM neural network which could be more robust to 
the external disturbance. 
1.2. Motivation and innovation 
Back to the data-based method, some of the prognostics methods are 
just focusing on the short-term forecasting field by introducing adaptive 
techniques [8–9,12–13,39]. In [40], the LSTM network was adopted to 
achieve high accuracy in single-step ahead prediction, while the RUL 
was defined as the difference value between the time to predict and the 
End-of-Life (EOL) point of the prediction curve [15]. In the aspect of 
long-term forecasting, the RUL prediction is achieved with iteration 
predictions generally. On the one hand, this recursive strategy may lead 
to an accumulation of errors by introducing the predicted voltage as the 
training input. On the other hand, the use of the operation parameters 
(such as current, inlet, and outlet temperature) as inputs of the prog­
nostic model is a hypothesis that states that the future information is 
known as prior knowledge, which is not always available in reality 
[20,41–42]. 
In this paper, the voltage time-series datasets are used to describe the 
aging process of PEMFCs. The LSTM network is used to fulfill short-term 
prognostic and life prediction. Secondly, the hyper-parameters adjust­
ment method based on the evolutionary algorithm (EA) is used. 
Furthermore, the accuracy of different multi-step ahead short-term 
prognostics strategies of PEMFCs are compared, and to find the most 
suitable strategy. Moreover, the proposed Multi-step Input and Multi- 
step Output (MIMO) strategy is extended into the long-term prognos­
tics field and implement RUL prediction at the same time.  
(1) Based on the LSTM network, the short-term prognostics of five 
multi-step ahead forecasting strategies are discussed under two 
PEMFC experiment datasets with the stationary and dynamic 
current.  
(2) A hyper-parameters adjustment method based on the EA is 
deployed to improve the forecasting performance, including the 
minimum batch size, the training epoch, and the cell numbers of 
the hidden layers.  
(3) The degradation trends of two stacks are characterized by the 
variable-step long-term prognostics method with the MIMO 
strategy. Besides, the RUL prediction is achieved under this 
prognostics framework. 
1.3. Organization 
The core of the paper is organized as follows. A description of the raw 
data source, the experimental setup, and the performed test can be found 
in Section 2. In section 3, the prediction method based on the LSTM is 
briefly outlined at first. Then, five multi-step short-term forecasting 
strategies of PEMFC are introduced. At last, a variable-step long-term 
forecasting method is extended from the MIMO strategy. In section 4, 
the results of different short-term prognostic strategies, the variable-step 
long-term prognostic, and RUL prediction are discussed. Finally, con­
clusions are presented. 
2. Experiment and dataset analysis 
In this section, a brief introduction of PEMFC operation principles is 
given at first. Then, the key information of the aging experiment settings 
and the aging dataset preprocessing is presented. 
2.1. Fundamentals of the PEMFC 
As an electrochemical device, the principle and the composition of 
the fuel cell are shown in Fig. 1 using oxygen and hydrogen to produce 
electricity, water, and heat. The global reaction equation of the system 
is: 
2H2 + O2→2H2O + electricity + heat

Due to the limitation on the capacity and the rated power, cells are 
generally integrated into a stack in series to cover the electricity de­
mand. As a power source, the stack should be equipped with different 
auxiliaries to implement gas supplement, control, and power output.

measured and controlled by the homemade interface in the LabVIEW®

3. The framework of the prognostics method 
The overall framework of the prognostic method put forward in this 
paper is shown in Fig. 3. The LSTM network is introduced to fulfill the 
multi-step short-term prognostic first, and the prediction performance of 
five different strategies is compared under two experiment datasets of 
PEMFCs from different aging tests. Furthermore, the variable step long- 
term aging prognostic is achieve based on the MIMO strategy. Both 
short-term and long-term prognostics are conducted under the EA, 
which helps to adjust the high parameters of the network instead of the 
manual regulation. 
3.1. LSTM network model 
Facing the problem of long-term dependencies, LSTMs are specif­
ically designed to avoid this issue as a variant of the recurrent neural 
network. Thus, remembering information for a long time is their 
inherent ability, rather than learning from model tuning and training. 
Due to the chain-like property, LSTM is available in time series predic­
tion, such as the data type of sequences and lists. As a result, LSTM has 
been used in speech recognition, language modeling, translation, image 
captioning, and other scenarios. 
The cell structure of LSTM is shown in Fig. 4. The key to LSTM is that 
the previous cell state Ct−1 to the current cell state Ct can be maintained 
under the tiny linear interactions. As a result, the cell state preserves 
valid information over long time scales. To be more specific, the LSTM 
can remove the old information and add something new, through the 
structures called gates, including forget gate, input gate, and output 
gate. The forget gate can be written as:

3.2. Multi-step ahead short-term forecasting strategies 
This section will first give a presentation of the five forecasting 
strategies. Several multi-step ahead forecasting strategies for time series 
have been proposed and summarized according to relative literature 
[44], which have been introduced into the multi-step short-term prog­
nostics of the PEMFC system.  
(1) Recursive strategy 
In this strategy, only one single model f is trained to realize a one- 
step-ahead prediction. ̂

ahead forecast, the predicted value is added in the previous step into the 
same model f for the forecasting of the next step. Thus, this process is 
executed until every step is forecasted.  
(2) Direct strategy 
Different from the recursive strategy, the direct strategy forecasts 
each horizon independently with H models gi as follows. ̂

larger computational burden compared with other strategies, and the 
complexity is proportional to the prognostics horizon length.

4.3. RUL prediction comparison 
The RUL time here is considered as the time between the prediction 
time and the failure time. And the RUL prediction result of the proposed 
method is compared with the EKF method. Meanwhile, several points 
are used to construct these evolutions. The RUL prediction results of FC1 
and FC2 based on the variable-step long-term method and EKF method 
are shown in Figs. 16 and 17. 
To obtain satisfactory results, the estimated RUL should be within 
the error bounds of the real RUL. In Fig. 16, the estimated RUL of the 
variable-step MIMO-LSTM method is within the bounds ±5% of the real 
RUL, while the estimated RUL of EKF is out of the bounds until the last 
estimation. As shown in Fig. 17, the estimated RUL of FC2 based on the 
variable-step MIMO-LSTM method is within the bounds of the real RUL 
as well. However, the RUL results remain more errors compared with the 
FC1. Especially for the RUL estimation of FC2 based on EKF, there 
remain large fluctuations in the forecasting points which are out of the 
±10% bounds. The reason is that the state equation of the EKF is 
motivated by the linear evolution of the aging parameters, while the 
system aging parameters shall contain more nonlinear factors under the 
dynamic current load. Thus, the RUL prediction performance of FC2 
based on the EKF is not satisfied. 
5. Conclusion 
This paper has proposed a data-based prognostics framework to 
achieve the multi-step ahead short-term forecasting and the variable- 
step long-term prediction at the same time. The proposed framework 
can provide a concise forecasting of PEMFC output performance without 
the future information as prior knowledge. First, five multi-step strate­
gies based on an LSTM structure are developed and compared by two 
data sets from the stationary and dynamic aging process. The compari­
son results figure out that the MIMO strategy is the most feasible strategy 
for the multi-step short-term prognostics of PEMFC. For the FC1 and FC2 
in this study, the best short-term prognostics performance yielded by the 
MIMO-LSTM in terms of RMSE* is 0.00532 and 0.00538, respectively, 
and in terms of MAE* is 0.00471 and 0.00491, respectively. Then, a 
hyper-parameters adjustment method is proposed to improve the 
training parameters of network structure. A variable-step long-term 
prognostics method is subsequently investigated based on the MIMO- 
LSTM. Moreover, the model-based EKF prognostics are compared with 
the proposed long-term prognostics method. The results indicate that 
the proposed RUL estimation accuracy can be improved to 95% and 90% 
confidence intervals for FC1 and FC2, respectively. Therefore, the pro­
posed short- and long-term prognostics framework has a significant 
performance in the short-term, long-term, and RUL prediction. How­
ever, faced with a more complex operating condition and aging process, 
a data-driven prediction method is difficult to be directly transferred 
into the specific application. Furthermore, future work can be extended 
to combine with the model-based method to expand its adaptability, in 
particular to solve the developed data-based method drawback of dy­
namic performance forecasting relatively low accuracy.
