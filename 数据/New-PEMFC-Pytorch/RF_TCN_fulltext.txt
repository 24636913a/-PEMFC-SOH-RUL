A B S T R A C T
Data-driven methods are effective in predicting future degradation trends (FDT) and remaining useful life (RUL) 
of proton exchange membrane fuel cells (PEMFCs). However, the complex and dynamic degradation behaviour 
of PEMFCs, influenced by diverse operational variables, poses significant challenges to existing prognostic ap­
proaches. This paper proposes a novel multivariable prognostic approach, termed RF-TCN, which combines 
random forest (RF) with temporal convolutional networks (TCN) to address these challenges. The approach 
incorporates three key innovations: (1) A hybrid RF and recursive feature elimination (RFE) method is employed 
to automatically select features most relevant to fuel cell degradation, reducing manual intervention and 
enhancing input robustness. (2) An improved TCN-based model is developed to effectively capture temporal 
degradation patterns, enabling accurate FDT and RUL predictions. (3) Particle swarm optimization (PSO) is 
utilized for automatic hyperparameter configuration, further boosting predictive performance. Empirical vali­
dation on ageing durability datasets demonstrates that the RF-TCN approach achieves superior prediction ac­
curacy with selected optimal features and outperforms baseline TCN, CNN, RNN, and existing methods in the 
literature. This work advances prognostic methodologies, contributing to extending fuel cell lifespan and opti­
mizing control strategies.
1. Introduction
1.1. Background

environmental protection, proton exchange membrane fuel cells 
(PEMFCs) are increasingly recognized as a vital component of future 
energy systems because of their ability to convert energy efficiently and 
cleanly [1,2]. However, their reliability and durability remain insuffi­
cient for large-scale commercial deployment [3–5]. Prognostics and

(FDT) and remaining useful life (RUL) by analyzing current and his­

threshold or fails to meet functional requirements [9]. Accurate pre­

allowing engineers to implement timely maintenance or adjust opera­
tional strategies to avoid sudden failures and improve the durability of 
PEMFCs [10–12]. Therefore, extensive research has been dedicated to 
improving FDT and RUL prediction accuracy, optimizing fuel cell 
management and extending their lifespan.
1.2. Literature review
PEMFC degradation prognostics are commonly addressed using 
model-based, data-driven, and fusion-based approaches [13,14]. Model- 
based methods rely on high-fidelity physical models [15], but their 
applicability is limited by the complexity and incomplete understanding 
of PEMFC degradation mechanisms [16]. Fusion-based methods, though 
fundamentally model-based, are challenging to model and require 
further theoretical development [17]. In contrast, data-driven methods 
have gained attention for their ability to predict PEMFC degradation 
directly from experimental data, eliminating the need for complex 
physical models [18]. Therefore, developing data-driven methods be­
comes a promising solution to addressing prediction challenges in fuel 
cell energy systems [9,19].
Currently, a variety of data-driven models have been applied to 
predict the FDT and RUL of PEMFCs. As summarized in Table 1, these 
models can be classified into two categories based on the number of 
input features: univariate and multivariable models. Univariate models 
[5,15,16,20–25], which primarily use stack voltage as the input, are 
computationally simple but fail to account for the influence of multiple 
operational variables. Additionally, the reliance on a single variable 
susceptible to noise diminishes prediction accuracy and reliability. By 
contrast, multivariable models [17–19,22,26–33] incorporate a range of 
operational parameters, such as current density, temperature, humidity, 
gas pressure, and fuel purity, showing more competitive and enabling 
more robust and accurate predictions, especially in complex, dynamic 
PEMFC systems [34,35]. However, despite their advancements, several 
critical challenges persist.
First, many studies rely on manually selected input features 
[19,27–31,33], which may result in inappropriate feature subsets and 
adversely affect prediction performance. This issue is critical, as the 
performance of data-driven methods largely depends on the selection of 
input features [36,37]. For instance, Zhang et al. [17] used the 
maximum information coefficient and Akaike information criterion 
(MIC-AIC) method to automate feature selection. However, their 
approach included performance output variables of individual cells, 
which limits its ability to fully exploit operational variables, and 
measuring these variables in practice is challenging [38]. Ensemble 
methods that integrate multiple single-feature selection algorithms [39], 
such as support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM- 
RFE) [40] and random forest-recursive feature elimination (RF-RFE) 
[41], have demonstrated improved accuracy in areas like gene selection 
for cancer classification [40] and molecular biology dataset analysis 
[41]. Nevertheless, these methods have not yet been applied to multi­
variable predictive models for fuel cells. RF-RFE combines the strengths 
of RF in handling high-dimensional data with complex nonlinear re­
lationships and providing fair feature importance assessments [42], 
alongside the iterative elimination capability of RFE. Leveraging these 
advantages, this study employs RF-RFE to identify features correlated 
with fuel cell degradation while excluding performance output variables 
of individual cells to fully utilize operational variables.
Second, most existing methods are primarily based on convolutional 
neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs), which 
have inherent limitations [14]. CNNs are inefficient at capturing long 
temporal dependencies due to the limitations of convolution kernel size 
[6,11], while RNNs suffer from short-term memory issues, vanishing and 
exploding gradients, and high computational costs [14,23]. To track 
these shortcomings, various strategies have been explored, such as 
integrating attention mechanisms into CNN/RNN architectures 
[18,19,29] and creating ensemble learning-based stacking models [17]. 
However, attention mechanisms generally increase model complexity 
[18,23], while stacking models face challenges in reducing model 
redundancy [44]. Transformer-based models, despite their exceptional 
performance in natural language processing, computer vision, and 
speech processing [14], demand significantly larger datasets and greater 
computational resources, limiting their applicability in data-constrained 
scenarios [45]. In contrast, the temporal convolutional network (TCN) is 
specifically designed for time series modelling [46]. By utilizing dilated 
causal convolutions, TCN naturally captures long-term dependencies in 
time series data while maintaining lower computational complexity 
compared to transformer-based models [14,46]. Additionally, TCN of­
fers a flexible architecture adaptable to diverse prediction tasks.

Third, configuring hyperparameters is a vital aspect of optimizing 
data-driven models [25,50], as manually or empirically determined 
parameters often fail to deliver optimal performance. Common tech­

intelligent optimization algorithms [15,17,25,33]. Among these, intel­

its simplicity, efficient search capability, and effective use of memory, is 
well-suitable for optimizing complex models [15,33]. This study em­

are derived from existing literature, with the optimization process 
conducted on validation datasets. The final model performance is

1.3. Research gap and contributions
The prediction accuracy of existing multivariable methods for 
PEMFC systems is limited by several limitations: First, reliance on 
manual feature selection often results in suboptimal subsets, compro­
mising predictive performance. Second, conventional CNNs and RNNs, 
commonly used in prognostic modelling, struggle to capture long-term 
dependencies and complex temporal patterns due to architectural con­
straints. Third, empirical hyperparameter tuning often yields subopti­
mal configurations, reducing model accuracy.
To address these challenges, this paper proposes a novel multivari­
able prognostic approach that combines RF and TCN, termed RF-TCN, to 
enhance the prediction accuracy of FDT and RUL for PEMFC systems. 
The approach introduces three core components: (1) The RF-RFE 
method is employed for the automated selection of features most rele­
vant to degradation, minimizing manual intervention and improving 
input robustness. (2) An improved TCN architecture is developed to 
effectively capture complex temporal degradation patterns and long- 
term dependencies. (3) PSO is employed to automate hyperparameter 
tuning, ensuring optimal configurations and improved model 
performance.
The key innovations and contributions of this study are as follows: 
(1) This study is the first to apply RF-RFE for PEMFC systems, 
enabling automated selection of optimal features and achieving 
higher accuracy compared to single-feature or all-feature 
configurations.
(2) Enhanced strategies are introduced into the baseline TCN, 
resulting in a predictive model for fuel cells that effectively 
captures long-term dependencies and improves computational 
efficiency.
(3) PSO ensures optimal hyperparameter configurations, reducing 
the need for manual tuning and improving predictive 
performance.
(4) The proposed approach is validated using two durability datasets. 
Results confirm that the proposed approach outperforms baseline 
TCN, CNN, and RNN models, achieving superior prediction ac­
curacy and robustness.

comparison, and analysis of the proposed approach. Finally, Section 5

2. Temporal convolutional network
The temporal convolutional network (TCN), developed by Bai et al. 
[46], is designed for time sequence modelling tasks. As presented in 
Fig. 1, the TCN architecture comprises causal convolution, dilated 
convolution, and residual blocks.
2.1. Dilated causal convolution
In basic causal convolutional networks, a 1D fully convolutional 
network with zero padding is employed to ensure that the length of each 
layer aligns with its preceding layer, as illustrated in Fig. 1(a). Addi­
tionally, causal convolutions are utilized to restrict operations at time t 
to inputs up to and including time t, effectively preventing future in­
formation leakage. However, tasks involving long historical data require 
deeper networks or larger filters, as the ability to retain historical in­
formation grows linearly with network depth and filter size. This results 
in greater model complexity and higher computational costs.
To overcome this challenge, dilated convolution layers are intro­
duced as an enhancement to causal convolution layers. By exponentially 
expanding the receptive field with network depth, dilated convolutions 
enable the efficient capture of long-term historical correlations without 
requiring excessively deep architectures [6]. For a 1D sequence of an

2.2. Residual connection
The residual network effectively addresses the gradient explosion 
and vanishing in deep architectures [48]. A residual block introduces a 
sequence of transformations F(x) added to the block’s input x, allowing 
layers to adjust the identity mapping instead of performing full trans­
formations [46]. The formulation is given by: 
y = Activation(x + F(x) )
(3) 
where y is the output, Activation(⋅) is the activation function, x is the 
input, and F(x) is the output of the convolution layer.
As depicted in Fig. 1 (b), a residual block includes two layers of 
dilated causal convolutions, weight normalization, Rectified Linear 
Units (ReLU), and dropout. The two middle components help accelerate 
training and enhance generalization [6], while the last component 
serves to regularize the model [53]. Additionally, a 1 × 1 convolution 
ensures that the identity mapping channel has the same width as the 
output [46], as illustrated in Fig. 1(c).
3. Proposed RF-TCN prognostic approach
As illustrated in Fig. 2, the RF-TCN prognostic approach comprises 
three sequential phases: feature selection, model construction and 
training, and model prediction and analysis.
In the first phase, the RF-RFE method is applied to select the features 
related to degradation from experimental monitoring variables. This 
iterative approach eliminates the least significant features in each step, 
ultimately producing an optimal feature subset. The second phase fo­
cuses on constructing and training a predictive model using an improved 
TCN architecture. Two improvement strategies are integrated to refine 
the baseline TCN, and the model is trained with the feature subset ob­
tained in the first phase. Additionally, the PSO algorithm is utilized to 
configure the model’s hyperparameters, ensuring optimal performance. 
In the final phase, the trained predictive model is employed to forecast 
PEMFC degradation and estimate RUL. The model’s predictive accuracy 
is validated against real-world data, and its performance is compared 
with traditional deep learning models to demonstrate the advantages of 
the proposed approach.

3.1.1. Data acquisition
Two durability datasets from the IEEE PHM 2014 data challenge, as 
detailed in [54,55], are utilized to verify the proposed approach. 
Detailed descriptions of these datasets are provided in subsection 4.1.
3.1.2. Feature importance assessment based on RF

decision trees to enhance the predictive precision and control model 
overfitting [42,56]. Known for its stability and ability to visualize var­
iable importance, RF is widely employed for selecting features in data- 
driven models [57]. Each tree is trained on a randomly sampled sub- 
dataset, and the final prediction is obtained by averaging individual 
tree output [58].
Feature importance is assessed through the Permutation Importance 
score, calculated by comparing the prediction accuracy before and after 
introducing noise to out-of-bag (OOB) data [58,59], as follows.

3.1.3. Feature selection using RF-RFE
Feature selection aims to identify the most informative features from 
the original feature space, improving the effectiveness and robustness of 
the predictors [36]. The RF-RFE method leverages RF’s ability to handle 
complex nonlinear relationships and RFE’s iterative elimination of un­
important features.
It is expected to reduce data redundancy and produce a more 
compact feature set, thereby enhancing the reliability and accuracy of 
feature selection. The RF-RFE feature selection process is illustrated in 
Fig. 3.
The implementation process is detailed below:
Step 1: Load and normalize the original feature set S0 with 18 var­
iables, as described in subsection 4.2, to form the current feature set SM 
= {s1, s2, …, sM}. Define Nf, the number of features in the updated subset 
SM-1, as the iteration criterion (Nf ≥1). Split the data into 70 % for 
training and 30 % for testing.
Step 2: Construct the RF regression model using SM. The number of 
decision trees is set to 100 [33], and the maximum number of splits uses 
the default automatic selection option in MATLAB. To improve model 
performance, apply ten-fold cross-validation during training.
Step 3: Compute the average mean square error (avgMSE) across ten 
iterations to evaluate model performance [38]. Simultaneously, calcu­
late the average importance score for each feature in SM over ten iter­
ations and rank the features in descending order of importance.
Step 4: Using the RFE algorithm, remove the least important feature 
with the lowest average importance score. Update the remaining feature 
set to SM-1 and construct a new regression model. Repeat Steps 2–4 until 
Nf = 1.
Step 5: Compare the avgMSE values recorded across all feature 
subsets S = {SM, SM-1, …, S1}. Identify the subset with the minimum 
avgMSE as the optimal feature subset S*, and output S*.
3.2. Model construction and training
This section describes the development and training process of the 
prediction model, as presented in Fig. 4. In the data preprocessing stage, 
median filtering is applied to reduce noise, followed by resampling to 
ensure adequate data representation. The processed data is standardized 
and split into training, validation, and testing sets. In the model con­
struction and training stage, an improved prediction model is developed 
by integrating two enhancement strategies into the baseline TCN ar­
chitecture, aiming to effectively capture temporal dependencies and 
represent features. The model is trained using the training set, while PSO 
is used to intelligently optimize hyperparameters, ensuring optimal 
performance. The prediction stage utilizes the optimized model to pre­
dict the FDT and RUL.

of PEMFCs as the monitoring dataset usually contains numerous raw 
data points and significant noise. To reduce computational costs and 
improve training efficiency, median filtering with a window size of 5

pattern. The filtered data are then resampled at half-hour intervals 
[17,30] to create new degradation datasets [15]. Before training the 
model, the resampled datasets are standardized using Eq. (5a) [23] and 
segregated into training, validation, and testing sets in specific pro­
portions. Notably, the validation set helps to assess the model’s perfor­
mance independently of the training and testing data [22].

3.2.2. New improved TCN-based prediction model
As described in subsection 2.1, the TCN architecture excels in 
handling time series prediction tasks. However, multivariate predictive 
models with higher dimensions require a wider receptive field to 
effectively capture long-term dependencies. This optimization involves 
adjusting the convolution filter size (k), dilation factor (d), and network 
depth (n), while considering computational costs [48]. To enhance the 
prediction performance of the baseline TCN architecture, two modifi­
cation strategies are implemented: 
(1) Four repeated convolution units are integrated into the original 
residual block structures to enable multi-layer feature extraction 
and accurate representation of local degradation properties. This 
approach improves feature extraction capabilities while avoiding 
the higher computational costs associated with stacking multiple 
residual blocks [48,49].
(2) A flattened layer and four fully connected layers are introduced in 
the regression stage to fuse extracted local features, providing a 
more comprehensive representation of PEMFC degradation.
The resulting improved TCN-based predictive model is presented in 
Fig. 5. The model comprises an input layer, a TCN layer, and a regression 
layer. Each repeated unit in the TCN layer is structured as Dilated Causal 
Conv + WeightNorm + ReLU + Dropout, with all convolution layers 
sharing the same filter size and a stride of one [6]. The dilation factors 
(d) for successive layers are set to 1, 2, 4, and 8, while dropout is applied 
only in the TCN layer. In the regression layer, the neuron counts for four 
fully connected layers are configured to 48, 32, 16, and 1, respectively 
[6].
3.2.3. Optimization technique
The PSO optimization process for the prediction model is illustrated 
in Fig. 6. First, a set of initial parameters for the prediction model is 
provided. Second, the PSO algorithm iteratively optimizes the hyper­
parameters to minimize the loss function value (fitness), which quan­
tifies the discrepancy between the target output and the predicted 
output on the validation sets [57]. This training process is repeated until 
the maximum number of iterations is reached. The trained model is 
applied to PEMFC prognostics, and its predictive performance is 
assessed using test sets.
The hyperparameters of the developed prediction model include the 
filter count (f), filter size (k), the number of residual blocks (n), and the 
dropout factor (D), where their search ranges are determined according 
to the results of existing literature as follows: f ∈[10, 64] [16,19], k ∈
[2, 17] [6,16,19], n ∈[1, 1], and D ∈[0.01, 0.5] [22,46]. Notably, the 
performance enhancement of the TCN is achieved by stacking multi- 
layer convolution units in the original residual block structure; thus, 
the number of residual blocks n is kept at 1. The hyperparameter opti­
mization process is implemented using MATLAB software, with the 
initial parameters specified as follows [25,51,52]: a population size of 
20, a maximum number of iterations of 50, a minimum inertia weight of 
0.4, a maximum inertia weight of 0.9, personal and global learning 
coefficients both set to 2 and a maximum particle velocity of 6.

3.3. Prediction and analysis
3.3.1. Degradation trend prediction method
PEMFC degradation trend prediction is a nonlinear time series task 
that requires constructing input-output patterns in a time series dataset 
[22]. This study employs the multi-step-ahead prediction method 
described in [19,23] to produce input-output data pairs for training, 
validation, and testing sets [25]. The method predicts several steps 
ahead by learning from earlier values, and after each prediction, the 
actual measurements are fed into the model [19]. This approach offers at 
least two advantages: maintaining a close input-output relationship and 
avoiding cumulative prediction errors [50].

4.4.1. FDT prediction results under different training lengths

proposed model. The grey straight line represents the points where

a high consistency agreement between predicted and real voltages.

demonstrate that the proposed model achieves high prediction accuracy 
and exhibits robust generalization, with its performance minimally 
impacted by variations in training length.

4.4.4. Comparison with other models
To further validate the effectiveness of the proposed RF-TCN 
approach, conventional deep learning models, including CNN, LSTM, 
and GRU, are employed to predict the FDT of PEMFC. In addition, 
predictions under different horizons (n steps ahead, with n = 5, 10, 15,

baseline TCN model [46], referred to as BTCN, is also implemented to 
assess the improvement of the improved TCN model. It is worth 
mentioning that the optimal feature set selected via the RF-RFE method 
is applied consistently across all models. The visual comparisons are 
depicted in Fig. 21 and Fig. 22 for FC1 and FC2, respectively, while 
Fig. 23 presents the comparative results of different models. MAPE and 
RMSE are used as evaluation metrics to ensure an unbiased comparison 
of prediction performances, as done in [18,19,65].
As depicted in Fig. 21 and Fig. 22, predictions begin at the 579th 
hour for FC1 and the 612th hour for FC2. The proposed RF-TCN 
approach best matches the real voltage degradation trends, followed 
by the BTCN model, which generally provides the second-best pre­
dictions. In contrast, the CNN model exhibits significant fluctuations, 
while the LSTM and GRU models observe substantial deviations from the 
actual degradation curves. Furthermore, compared to the results in [19] 
(Fig. 12 for FC1 and Fig. 13 for FC2 in [19]), which show significant 
irregular degradation trends and “late” predictions, the proposed 
approach more accurately captures the real degradation behaviour and 
provides “early” degradation predictions for the FC2 task, indicating 
that the proposed approach is suitable for handling long-time data, even 
with fluctuation in the raw data, and can identify early signs of degra­
dation of fuel cells.
Accurate and “early” predictions are particularly valuable for 
control-oriented ageing prediction problems, as they provide operators 
with sufficient lead time to optimize system performance. While the 
accuracy of the CNN/LSTM/GRU models could be improved with 
attention mechanisms [18,19], such enhancements would increase the 
model complexity [18,23]. In contrast, the proposed approach achieves 
superior prediction accuracy without requiring additional complexity, 
making it a more practical and efficient solution.
Fig. 23 shows that the prediction performance reduces as the pre­
diction horizon extends. This results from the models’ inability to cap­
ture both local and global features from the input sequences [23]. 
Despite this, the proposed RF-TCN approach achieves higher prediction 
accuracy than all other models, including the attention-based dilated 
CNN developed in Ref. [19]. Moreover, compared to the RMSE reported 
in [66] for 10 steps ahead prediction horizon (0.00730 for FC1 and 
0.00810 for FC2), the proposed RF-TCN approach obtains RMSE values 
of 0.00627 for FC1 and 0.00765 for FC2, improving the accuracy by 
16.43 % and 5.88 %, respectively. Overall, it can be further concluded 
that the proposed RF-TCN approach is more capable of handling PEMFC 
prognostic tasks and is more valuable in practice.

5. Conclusions
This paper introduces a novel multivariable prognostic approach for 
PEMFCs, termed RF-TCN, which combines random forest and temporal 
convolutional networks. The proposed approach is validated using two 
durability test datasets and compared with existing literature results 
[19], the BTCN model [46], and three traditional deep learning models.
Firstly, a hybrid feature selection method integrating the advantages 
of RF and RFE algorithms is employed to automatically select optimal 
features. These features are then validated against existing mechanistic 
analysis results and subsequently fed into the improved TCN-based 
predictive model optimized using the PSO algorithm. The model per­
formance is evaluated under different training lengths and input feature 
sets, along with its multi-step ahead prediction capability. Results show 
that the model maintains high prediction accuracy across different 
training lengths, demonstrating robust generalization. When adopting 
optimal input features, the model achieves higher accuracy and reduced 
uncertainty compared to single-input and all-input models, verifying the 
effectiveness of selected features. Furthermore, the proposed approach 
outperforms the attention-based dilated CNN in [19], the BTCN model 
[46], and CNN, LSTM, and GRU models in PEMFC degradation predic­
tion problems for multi-step ahead forecasting. These results demon­
strate that the proposed approach is capable of effectively handling 
PEMFC prognostic tasks.
Overall, the data-driven prognostic approach developed in this work 
can precisely predict the FDT and RUL of fuel cells, which can assist in 
achieving intelligent decision-making processes for fuel cell systems, 
including real-time optimization of fault-tolerant control and manage­
ment strategies.
