A B S T R A C T
Prognostics and health management play a crucial role in ensuring the safety and maintenance of fuel cell ve­
hicles. To address the insufficient prediction accuracy in existing models when handling long-term sequences and 
high-dimensional data, this paper proposes a proton exchange membrane fuel cell (PEMFC) remaining useful life 
prediction method based on a matrix long short-term memory (M-LSTM) network. Specifically, the Spearman 
correlation coefficient analysis is first employed to quantitatively evaluate the multidimensional correlations 
within the experimental data, identify key variables highly associated with fuel cell degradation, optimize 
feature selection, and achieve dimensionality reduction. Subsequently, an M-LSTM life prediction model is 
developed, incorporating an exponential gating mechanism and a matrix storage unit to enhance the global 
modeling capability of data-driven methods in complex nonlinear feature learning and long-sequence data 
processing. The results demonstrate that, compared with the LSTM model, under single-step prediction, M-LSTM 
reduces MAE and MSE by 76.3% and 58.2%, respectively, while increasing R2 by 2.3%. Under multi-step pre­
diction, M-LSTM reduces MAE and MSE by 79.4% and 60.4% respectively, while improving R2 by 5.2%.
1. Introduction
In recent years, fuel cell vehicle technology using hydrogen as the 
main fuel is also considered to be one of the most important technical 
routes in the development of new energy vehicles [1–4]. Fuel cell ve­
hicles have a short hydrogen refueling time and strong endurance. The 
only reactant in its operation is water, which has almost no pollution to 
the environment. Therefore, using fuel cell vehicles is a favorable way to 
maintain a clean environment and protect human health [5–7]. 
Although after years of research and continuous development, the pro­
ton exchange membrane fuel cell (PEMFC) installed in automobiles has 
made great research progress in terms of volume, low-temperature 
start-up, and energy efficiency, its durability problem has not yet 
reached the goal of industrialization and marketization [8,9]. Therefore, 
to accurately predict the degradation degree and remaining useful life 
(RUL) of PEMFC, extend the service life of fuel cells, and expand the 
application of fuel cell systems, prognostics, and health management 
methods have received widespread attention [10,11]. Currently, 
prediction methods are mainly divided into three categories: 
model-driven methods, data-driven methods, and hybrid models 
[12–14].
Model-based prediction methods can be divided into the use of 
degradation mechanism models, empirical models, or semi-empirical 
models [15]. Futter et al. [16] simulated the degradation process of 
the proton exchange membrane by introducing various chemical 
mechanisms in the battery, and verified the accuracy of the degradation 
model through an accelerated stress test (AST). This model belongs to a 
type of mechanism model. The introduction of chemical mechanisms 
greatly increases the complexity of modeling. Ou et al. [17] proposed a 
semi-empirical model-based prognostic method for predicting the 
degradation and remaining useful life of PEMFCs. The approach in­
tegrates a voltage prediction model, aging parameter analysis, and 
performance recovery after shutdown, validated through experimental 
cases. Although the semi-empirical model can reduce the complexity of 
modeling, the empirical data in it needs to be accumulated through 
experiments. Zhou et al. [18] proposed a real-time prediction (RTP)

operating conditions, estimating voltage decay rates for each, and con­

models and are therefore classified here as a special type of model-based 
approach. Pan et al. [19] introduced a novel hybrid technique 
combining an adaptive Kalman filter and a data-driven NARX neural 
network, where the Kalman filter and empirical aging model capture 
macroscopic degradation trends, while the NARX neural network pro­

adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system, enabling long-term degradation 
trend prediction under variable current loads and remaining life esti­
mation. Wang et al. [21] developed a hybrid model that refines the input 
of a data-driven model using semi-empirical outputs, significantly 
reducing oscillations in long-term degradation predictions. However, 
fuel cell systems are complex physical and multi-scale systems. It is a 
difficult task to establish an accurate degradation model. In addition, 
multi-dimensional models require a lot of computing resources and 
time, which makes it difficult to apply to dynamic control and online 
diagnosis of fuel cells [22].
Therefore, many scholars have proposed data-driven prediction 
methods for fuel cell systems. Data-driven prediction methods refer to 
learning the degradation behavior of fuel cells from fuel cell aging 
experimental data through statistics or machine learning and using these 
learning results to predict the life of fuel cells [23]. Compared with 
model-based prediction methods, data-driven prediction methods can 
achieve high prediction accuracy without analyzing the degradation 
mechanism inside the fuel cell. LSTM is a special type of RNN (Recurrent 
Neural Network) designed to solve the shortcomings of RNN in dealing 
with long-term dependency problems. [24–26], which can avoid the 
problems of gradient explosion and vanishing. The fuel cell prediction 
method based on the LSTM architecture is currently a relatively effective 
means. Liu et al. [27] proposed a new method for predicting the 
remaining useful life (RUL) of PEMFC, which used an LSTM-based 
recurrent neural network combined with data smoothing and recon­
struction techniques to improve prediction accuracy and reliability. Jia 
et al. [28] proposed a novel data-driven prediction model that integrates 
the multi-head self-attention (MHSA) mechanism and bidirectional long 
short-term memory.
However, LSTM also has some problems. First, the original intention 
of LSTM design is to overcome the long-term dependency problem of 
traditional RNN, but when processing very long sequences, LSTM may 
still perform poorly in capturing very long-distance dependencies. In 
particular, when the length of the sequence data exceeds a certain range, 
its performance will drop significantly. Second, LSTM needs to process 
sequence data time step by time, which makes it difficult to achieve 
efficient parallel computing. Compared with models such as Trans­
former, LSTM has weaker parallel computing capabilities on large-scale 
data, which limits its application in certain high-dimensional, large- 
scale tasks. In recent studies, Beck et al. [30] proposed a Matrix of Long 
Short-Term Memory (M-LSTM). Exponential gating is used in M-LSTM to 
efficiently modify stored values when more relevant information is 
encountered, overcoming a significant limitation of traditional LSTM. 
Matrix memory provides greater storage capacity, enabling M-LSTM to 
more efficiently handle rare labels, long-range dependencies, and 
complex data patterns. Finally, M-LSTM is fully parallelizable, enabling 
it to compute efficiently on modern hardware accelerators and scale to 
larger models. Table 1 is a comparison of the advantages and disad­
vantages of various life prediction methods.
Considering that fuel cell life data has the characteristics of high data 
dimension and high complexity, the computational requirements of 
neural networks are very high. Therefore, this paper studies a fuel cell 
life prediction method based on M-LSTM to alleviate the problem of 
large prediction result errors caused by the high dimension and high 
complexity of fuel cell life prediction data. Thanks to the exponential 
gating, matrix characteristics and parallelization of M-LSTM, the 
accuracy of fuel cell life prediction is improved.

2. Experiments and data processing
The fuel cell system is mainly composed of an air supply system, a 
hydrogen supply system, a water circulation system, and a high-voltage 
circuit, as shown in Fig. 2. The fuel cell platform used in this experiment 
has a rated power of 90 kW.
The stack consists of 400 cells connected in series. The experimental 
environment was set to standard atmospheric pressure, 25 ◦C, and 50% 
humidity. When measuring the polarization curve, the operating voltage 
range of the fuel cell stack was 0.46 V–0.75 V, and the current density 
varied from 0 A/cm2 to 1 A/cm2.The following provides an overview of 
each component:① Gas Supply System: The gas supply system ensures a 
continuous supply of reactant gases during the operation of the PEMFC 
stack. In this experiment, it is divided into a hydrogen supply unit and an 
air supply unit. Hydrogen is delivered from storage tanks, while air is 
compressed, filtered, and stored before being provided. ② Cooling 
System: The cooling system maintains the PEMFC stack’s operating 
temperature within the optimal range, typically 30 ◦C–80 ◦C. At the start 
of the operation, when the stack temperature is low, the water tank 
automatically heats deionized water, which is circulated through the 
stack by a water pump to raise the temperature. Conversely, when the 
stack temperature becomes too high, the circulating water is cooled via 
cooling pipes and pumps to stabilize the system. ③ Electronic Load: To 
evaluate the performance and degradation of the PEMFC stack under 
real-world conditions, external electronic loads with higher current, 
voltage, and power ratings than the stack are employed. These loads 
simulate actual operating conditions and meet the requirements of the 
experimental design.
The fuel cell life data acquisition process is shown in Fig. 3. The life 
test conditions include four conditions: idle, start-stop, rated, and vari­
able load. The experiment was carried out on a fuel cell bench, and a 
total of 1374 h of data was acquired. By plotting the polarization curve 
of the fuel cell, we can see that at the same current density, the fuel cell 
voltage begins to decrease over time, which shows that voltage is suit­
able as a parameter for characterizing fuel cell aging. In the following, 
the aging of the fuel cell will be evaluated by the decline of the fuel cell 
voltage. Considering that the current density of the fuel cell is 0.7 A/cm2 
when it is working, the voltage aging data when the current density is 
0.7 A/cm2 in the polarization curve is selected as the voltage aging data 
set.

accuracy. To address this, a wavelet thresholding technique is employed

an MSE of 0.3138 × 10−5, and an R2 of 0.9982. Compared to LSTM, M-

When comparing the results between the 50% and 70% training sets, 
M-LSTM maintains its high performance across both datasets. With the 
70% training set, M-LSTM achieved an MAE of 0.5076 × 10−3, MSE of 
0.1464 × 10−5, and R2 of 0.9997. This is only a slight improvement over 
the 50% training set, where it achieved an MAE of 0.5183 × 10−3, MSE 
of 0.2556 × 10−5, and R2 of 0.9993. Specifically, the decrease in MAE is 
just 0.0107 × 10−3, in MSE is 0.1090 × 10−5, and in R2 is 0.0004. This 
minimal loss of accuracy with the reduced dataset highlights M-LSTM’s 
strong generalization ability, allowing it to effectively maintain high 
prediction performance even with less training data.
The article further compares the performance of each model under 
single-step and multi-step prediction. By comparing Figs. 10 and 11, 
Figs. 13 and 14, it can be found that the prediction performance of all 
models has declined in multi-step prediction, but the results show that 
the prediction performance of M-LSTM is still higher than that of the 
other three models, and the prediction results will not have a large de­
viation due to multi-step prediction. On the contrary, due to the limi­
tations of its model structure, the accuracy of LSTM has dropped 
significantly in multi-step prediction.
In summary, the comparison results show that M-LSTM has good 
prediction performance, and its prediction accuracy is higher than 
LSTM, LSTM-Transformer and S-LSTM. There are three main reasons: 
First, M-LSTM uses matrices to store key-value pairs, which is similar to 
the attention mechanism of Transformer and can capture richer data 
features. Second, the covariance update rule enables M-LSTM to auto­
matically learn key-value relationships, avoids the design of complex 
feature extractors, and effectively captures long-distance dependencies. 
Finally, M-LSTM uses the parallelism of matrix operations to accelerate 
training and inference, which is particularly suitable for high- 
dimensional data processing. The aging data of PEMFC, including 
voltage, current, temperature, etc., often depends on longer time series 
data and has higher data dimensions. The above characteristics of M- 
LSTM are highly matched with the characteristics of PEMFC aging data, 
so better prediction results are obtained.
5. Conclusion
This paper proposes a prediction model based on matrix long short- 
term memory (M-LSTM) network for the problem of PEMFC remaining 
service life prediction, and verifies its effectiveness through experi­
ments. The main research results are summarized as follows. 
1) This paper uses the Spearman correlation coefficient to reduce the 
dimensionality of experimental data, identify variables that are 
highly correlated with PEMFC voltage decay, effectively reduce data 
redundancy, and improve model calculation efficiency and predic­
tion performance.
2) The M-LSTM model effectively alleviates the performance degrada­
tion of the traditional LSTM model when processing long-term 
sequence data and high-dimensional data sets. Experimental results 
show that the M-LSTM model is superior to the LSTM and LSTM- 
Transformer models in terms of prediction accuracy and stability, 
especially when dealing with sparse labels, long-distance de­
pendencies, and complex data patterns. Specifically, compared with 
the LSTM model, under single-step prediction, M-LSTM reduces MAE 
and MSE by 76.3% and 58.2%, respectively, while increasing R2 by 
2.3%. Under multi-step prediction, M-LSTM reduces MAE and MSE 
by 79.4% and 60.4% respectively, while improving R2 by 5.2%.
Compared with traditional LSTM, M-LSTM introduces additional 
computational overhead due to the matrix memory mechanism. In 
PEMFC life prediction with high real-time requirements, especially 
when rapid decision-making or dynamic adjustment is required, the 
computational complexity of M-LSTM may become a bottleneck. In the 
future, the model architecture can be further optimized, such as com­
pressing the M-LSTM model or adopting lightweight deep learning 
methods to reduce the demand for computing resources and improve the 
efficiency of real-time prediction.
